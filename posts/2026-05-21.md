## LinkedIn Post

In-pipeline time for B2B deals shortened this year. From 62 days to 52.

That should be a sign of sales efficiency. In most cases, it isn't.

Dreamdata's 2026 LinkedIn Ads Benchmarks Report — drawn from 66 million sessions across 3.5 million customer journeys — shows the average B2B buying journey stretched from 211 to 272 days. But that growth happened entirely before the sales pipeline began. Buyers now spend a median 220 days in self-directed research before ever entering a CRM. They arrive at SQL with vendor shortlists largely formed.

The pipeline is closing faster because buyers arrive more decided — not because sales got better at closing.

That's a structural problem for demand generation. If attribution models start at MQL or pipeline entry, most programs are capturing the last 19% of buyer influence time. The preceding 81% is happening in content they consume but don't click, communities they read but don't join, AI tools they use but don't surface.

A separate 2026 analysis (Landbase) found 94% of buying groups have already ranked preferred vendors before first contact. Stakeholders per deal have grown from 6.8 to 10 — influence is more distributed, and it peaks earlier.

This starts looking less like an attribution gap and more like a scope problem. Demand generation was designed to generate pipeline. The buying process has moved upstream.
