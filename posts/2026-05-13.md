## LinkedIn Post

Two data sets published within days of each other seem to contradict each other.

The Datos Q1 2026 State of Search report (Semrush / Rand Fishkin) found that zero-click searches in the US actually declined in Q1 — from 24.5% in December to 22.4% in March, the lowest level on record. Organic click share rose, not fell.

At the same time, a randomized field experiment posted to SSRN by researchers at Carnegie Mellon and the Indian School of Business found that Google AI Overviews cut organic clicks by 38% on the queries where they appear. Zero-click behavior jumped from 54% to 72% when an Overview was shown.

Both findings are correct. The reconciliation is where the strategy lives.

AI Overviews don't appear on all queries — the experiment puts it at roughly 42%. On the queries they do appear, the click loss is severe and now causally proven. But Google's overall search volume is growing, which can absorb some of that loss in aggregate. The macro signal looks calm. The query-level damage is concentrated.

For B2B technology, this distinction matters more than in almost any other industry: AI Overviews appear on 70% of B2B tech queries, the highest rate across all sectors (Stackmatix, 2026). And only 38% of the pages cited in AI Overviews also rank in the top 10 for the same query — meaning organic rankings and AI citation are now two separate tracks, not one.

This starts to look less like a traffic problem and more like a coverage problem. Teams optimizing organic and paid without separately managing citation presence are leaving the highest-exposure queries unaddressed — exactly the informational queries where B2B buyers are forming shortlists before they ever reach a vendor's site.

Source: Datos Q1 State of Search 2026 — https://datos.live/report/state-of-search-q1-2026/ | SSRN field experiment — https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6513059

## Topic Selection

## Recommended Topic
The conflict between the Datos Q1 2026 finding that organic clicks are recovering and the SSRN causal experiment showing AI Overviews cut clicks 38% on queries where they appear — and what this means for B2B demand generation strategy.

## Selected Angle
Analytical / Skeptical

## Central Insight
Two rigorous data sources published within days of each other tell opposite stories about AI's impact on organic search — and both are correct. The reconciliation reveals something more strategically useful than either headline: AI Overviews hurt you severely on the queries where they appear (38% click reduction), but the aggregate effect is smaller than feared because (a) AI Overviews don't appear on every query, and (b) Google's total search volume is growing. The real demand gen implication isn't "organic is dying" — it's that discovery is fragmenting into citation-eligible queries and click-through queries, and optimizing for only one of those tracks is a structural gap.

## Why This Matters
- B2B tech sees AI Overviews on 70% of queries, meaning the citation-eligible category is larger here than in most industries
- Only 38% of pages cited in AI Overviews also rank in the top 10 — so paid search and organic SEO alone don't guarantee citation coverage
- The counter-narrative (organic clicks recovering overall) is likely to create complacency about AI visibility, while the per-query click loss is severe for the informational, high-intent queries where B2B buyers form shortlists
