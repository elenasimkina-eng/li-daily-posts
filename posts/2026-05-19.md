## LinkedIn Post

LinkedIn and Bain ran a buyability study and found that 81% of won B2B deals had broad awareness across the full buying group from day one. When only the champion's function knew the vendor — the typical outcome of a persona-targeted demand gen program — win rate dropped to 4%.

That is not a performance gap. That is a structural flaw.

Most demand gen programs are built around reaching the right person: the decision-maker, the IT buyer, the economic champion. ICP definitions, LinkedIn targeting, content strategy, ABM segmentation — all of it centers on persona precision. The logic feels sound. Find the person most likely to buy, influence them, and trust the handoff to sales.

What the data suggests is that this architecture optimizes for something the deal doesn't actually depend on. The champion is rarely the problem. The committee is. And if the buying group arrives at the evaluation stage having never encountered the vendor — only the champion has — the vendor is entering a credibility deficit with 7 to 15 people who haven't been warmed.

The 4% win rate isn't a failure of the champion. It's a consequence of treating demand generation as a targeting problem rather than a coverage problem.

Kaseya's 2026 State of the MSP Report adds context: 71% of MSPs cite customer acquisition as their top challenge this year (n=1,061). Deal compression is happening across the market. The irony is that the most common response to pipeline pressure is to sharpen persona targeting further — tighten the ICP, reduce waste, improve qualification. Which is precisely the move that leaves the buying group in the dark.

This starts looking less like a lead quality problem and more like a presence architecture problem. The vendors winning in 2026 may not be the ones with the most refined targeting — they may be the ones whose brand is already familiar to everyone in the room before the evaluation starts.

## Topic Selection

## Recommended Topic
The structural collapse of persona-based demand generation, revealed by the 20x win rate gap between multi-function and single-function buying group awareness.

## Selected Angle
Analytical

## Central Insight
Most B2B demand gen programs are architected around reaching the right persona — the IT buyer, the CISO, the operations lead. LinkedIn's Buyability study with Bain shows that architecture is wrong at a structural level: 81% of won deals had broad awareness across the full buying group from day one; deals where only the champion's function knew the vendor closed at 4%. Demand gen designed around persona precision is inadvertently sabotaging pipeline by leaving the rest of the buying group dark.

## Why This Matters
- The persona-targeting model underpins most B2B demand gen execution — ICP definition, LinkedIn targeting, content strategy, and ABM tiers — and this data suggests the model optimizes for the wrong outcome
- The 20x win rate gap (81% vs. 4%) is not a marginal performance difference; it signals a structural flaw in programs that reach the champion but not the committee
- For cybersecurity vendors selling to MSPs, where buying decisions often involve technical, commercial, and ownership stakeholders with different objections, this pattern is especially high-impact
