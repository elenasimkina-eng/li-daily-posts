## LinkedIn Post

Win rates in ABM peak at three buying groups. Add three more, and you've cut them in half.

That's from Demandbase's State of ABM 2026 report, built on 9.7 million sales interactions across 1,452 organizations (https://www.demandbase.com/resources/labs/state-of-abm-2026-benchmark-report/). Teams focused on three buying groups average ~29% win rates. Teams tracking six average ~12%.

The instinct to broaden is understandable. Bigger buying committees mean more consensus to build, more stakeholders with sign-off authority, more reasons to add another persona to the program. The data suggests each addition introduces competing priorities and diluted messaging — not better coverage.

What's interesting is how this creates a measurement trap. Most ABM programs track engagement breadth: accounts touched, personas reached, sequences initiated. Those metrics actively incentivize the behavior that drives win rates down.

ABM generates 2.6x more pipeline per marketing dollar than broad-reach demand gen. But that advantage is conditional on precision — the same precision teams systematically give up when they try to scale.

More buying groups doesn't scale ABM — it averages it.

## Topic Selection

## Recommended Topic
How buying group scope functions as the primary variable in ABM win rate — and why expanding coverage systematically undermines performance.

## Selected Angle
Analytical

## Central Insight
ABM win rates peak when teams focus on 2–3 buying groups per product and collapse as coverage expands — meaning that attempts to scale ABM by broadening stakeholder coverage destroy the precision mechanism that makes ABM outperform broad-reach demand gen in the first place.

## Why This Matters
- Most ABM programs are measured on engagement breadth (accounts touched, personas reached, sequences sent), which actively incentivizes the expansion behavior that reduces win rates
- The data reveals a structural constraint: ABM's 2.6x pipeline advantage over broad-reach programs is conditional on precision, not coverage
- For demand gen teams in cybersecurity and MSP markets — where buying committees are now 6–10 stakeholders — the instinct to add more buying groups to ABM programs may be worsening the very problem they're trying to solve

## Key Signals
- Win rates average ~29% at 3 buying groups and fall to ~12% at 6 buying groups — more than halved [Demandbase State of ABM 2026 — https://www.demandbase.com/resources/labs/state-of-abm-2026-benchmark-report/]
- Companies tracking 3–4 buying groups see 48.5% higher win rate vs. organizations with broader approaches [Demandbase State of ABM 2026 — https://www.demandbase.com/resources/labs/state-of-abm-2026-benchmark-report/]
- ABM-led programs generate 2.6x more pipeline per marketing dollar than broad-reach demand gen — but this depends on execution precision [Demandbase State of ABM 2026 — https://www.demandbase.com/resources/labs/state-of-abm-2026-benchmark-report/]
