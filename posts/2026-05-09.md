## LinkedIn Post

chiefmartec published the 2026 Marketing Technology Landscape Supergraphic this week — and the headline number is deceptively quiet.

15,505 total products. Up 0.79% from last year. After 15 years of relentless expansion, the martech market has effectively plateaued.

But the composition of that number is where the real signal lives.

In a single year, 1,488 new products entered the market while 1,367 were removed. Net addition of 121. That is not stagnation — it is replacement. The landscape looks flat because arrivals and exits are nearly canceling each other out.

Of the tools that exited, 51.7% came from the 2010–2019 SaaS wave. The first generation of cloud-native martech — the category that built the modern demand gen stack — is now the largest cohort of departures.

The categories gaining ground: AEO/GEO (Answer Engine Optimization), analytics, integration, and CMS infrastructure. These are precisely the capabilities that matter in an environment where B2B Technology queries now trigger AI Overviews 82% of the time, up from 36% just one year ago.

The practical tension for demand gen teams: the stack assembled over the last decade was optimized for a search and attribution environment that is structurally changing. The tools being retired are the ones that assumed organic traffic, form-based attribution, and linear funnels. The tools gaining share assume AI-mediated visibility and account-level signal aggregation.

The risk is not a sudden collapse. It is a slow drift in which existing infrastructure continues to run while the buyer environment it was built to serve no longer operates the same way.

## Central Insight
The martech market has not stagnated — it has entered a structural replacement cycle: 1,488 products were added while 1,367 were removed in a single year, and over half the exits came from tools built during the 2010–2019 SaaS expansion. The growth categories (AEO/GEO, analytics, integration) are precisely the infrastructure categories that support AI-era demand generation, while legacy campaign/automation tooling is the cohort being displaced.

## Why This Matters
- Demand gen teams relying on their existing martech stack without auditing against this generational churn risk running infrastructure that is actively being deprecated by the market
- The categories gaining share (AEO/GEO, analytics, integration) map directly to the capability gaps exposed by AI search and dark funnel attribution
- The surface-level stability (0.79% growth) creates a false sense that martech decisions can wait — the churn data says the opposite
