## LinkedIn Post

Most cybersecurity demand gen programs lead with threat data. That assumption is starting to break.

The CyberSmart MSP Survey 2026 (cybersmart.co.uk/msp-cybersecurity-report-2026) found that 46% of MSP customers now rank rising costs and inflation as their top concern. One year ago, that answer ranked fifth. Security risk didn't drop because the environment improved. Economic pressure moved up past it.

Nothing improved on the threat side. The same survey found 54% of MSPs were breached two or more times in the past 12 months.

Breach frequency isn't registering as urgency because the people evaluating cybersecurity spend have different math. Buying committees have expanded from 4-6 stakeholders to 6-10. More of those seats go to finance, operations, and procurement — people who don't run breach scenarios. They run budget comparisons.

77% of MSPs cite lack of client urgency as a major sales challenge. That's almost always framed as a sales execution problem. The data suggests the gap starts earlier, in demand gen messaging calibrated to a buyer priority order that changed this year.

Risk-led narratives still reach technical buyers. They don't survive the walk down the hall.

## Central Insight
Despite a worsening threat environment (54% of MSPs breached two or more times in the past year), 46% of MSP customers now rank rising operational costs above cybersecurity risk — a priority that jumped from fifth place in 2025 to first in 2026. Most cybersecurity demand gen still leads with breach risk and threat severity. The buyers evaluating those messages are now running a different calculation.

## Why This Matters
- Demand gen messaging built around risk is competing against economic anxiety that has moved up the priority stack faster than any threat signal
- 77% of MSPs cite lack of client urgency as a top sales challenge — this is usually treated as a sales problem, but the root sits upstream in how demand is generated and framed
- Buying committees have expanded from 4-6 to 6-10 stakeholders, meaning finance and operations now hold seats in the evaluation process, not just technical buyers
