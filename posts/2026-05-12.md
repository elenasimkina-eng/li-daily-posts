## LinkedIn Post

Gartner's March 2026 survey of 646 B2B buyers found that 67% prefer a rep-free purchase experience — up from 61% just eight months earlier. Separately, 45% said they used AI tools during a recent purchase.

Forrester's State of Business Buying 2026, drawing on 17,500+ global buyers, adds the context: average buying groups now include 13 internal stakeholders and 9 external participants. And buyers are "widening their networks to gather and validate information" specifically so they can defend the decision internally.

What stands out is the structural mismatch this creates for demand generation.

Most demand gen programs are still designed around one central objective: produce enough qualified volume that reps can engage, qualify, and advance pipeline. The funnel ends at handoff. Success is measured in MQLs, rep-accepted leads, and pipeline generated post-conversation.

But the majority of buyers are now making shortlisting decisions before that conversation exists. They're using AI tools, peer reviews, community mentions, and analyst references to build and narrow the list — without ever raising their hand.

Which means the demand gen investment driving MQL volume may have limited influence over a selection process that's already completed before it starts.

This starts looking less like a lead volume problem and more like a credibility distribution problem. The vendors appearing in AI-generated research, review site summaries, and community conversations during that self-directed evaluation phase may be anchoring the shortlist. The vendor that reaches the rep conversation stage may have already won or lost before the first SDR sequence fires.

For cybersecurity and MSP-focused vendors, the committee dynamics sharpen this further. Thirteen stakeholders, each doing their own AI-assisted research, each forming independent vendor impressions. A single rep touchpoint doesn't reach them. What reaches them is what surfaces when they search.

The assumption worth revisiting: that demand generation's primary job is to create rep engagement opportunities. It may increasingly be to ensure the brand is present and credible in the research environments buyers use before they ever consider engaging a rep.

## Topic Selection

## Recommended Topic
The structural mismatch between demand generation program design (optimized for rep engagement) and the documented preference of the majority of B2B buyers to complete research and shortlisting without rep contact.

## Selected Angle
Analytical

## Central Insight
Demand generation programs are still largely structured to create opportunities for sales rep engagement, but 67% of B2B buyers now prefer rep-free experiences, 45% are using AI tools during purchases, and buying groups have expanded to 13+ stakeholders — meaning the first vendor that earns credibility in AI-driven, self-directed research may be determining the shortlist before any rep interaction occurs.

## Why This Matters
- Programs measuring success by MQL handoff and rep-accepted pipeline may be measuring a stage the buyer is actively trying to skip
- Credibility signals that work in AI-driven self-directed research (review site citations, third-party mentions, expert community presence) are structurally different from those optimized for rep-led outreach
- For cybersecurity/MSP sales in particular — where buying committees include CISO, IT, compliance, finance, and procurement — the rep-free preference means 13+ stakeholders are forming views independently before a single sales conversation

Source: Growth Hackers — https://growthhackers.se/en/blog/meta-click-attribution-update-2026

Companies generating the most pipeline use a format mix and measure at the pipeline level, not the click level.
