## LinkedIn Post

The standard playbook says: prioritize accounts showing intent signals, activate fast, close the window. 87% of B2B organizations report unreliable or inflated intent signals, and only 26% have confidence in the data they're acting on.

That gap is worth sitting with.

MarTech recently made the case that the intent data playbook is breaking down (martech.org/the-intent-data-playbook-is-breaking-down). The core problem is the premise: most third-party intent signals measure research activity, not purchase readiness. A company visiting a pricing page isn't necessarily close to buying anything.

The accuracy problem compounds it. Match rates between anonymous browsing and company identity range from 40% to 85% depending on methodology, per Packed Data Services. Almost no provider resolves signals to the individual. ABM activation is mostly built on probabilistic company-level estimates.

31% of sales leaders now call intent data the most overrated technology in their stack.

That's not fringe skepticism. It's a widening split between what platforms sell and what sales teams see in the pipeline.

Intent data doesn't tell you who's buying. It tells you who's reading.

## Central Insight
Intent data platforms promise precision targeting by identifying "in-market" accounts, but the underlying methodology — inferring purchase readiness from research behavior — is probabilistic, company-level, and contested by the sales teams who are supposed to benefit. The data doesn't support the use case as marketed.

- Match rates of 40–85% mean a large fraction of ABM "precision" targeting is misdirected spend
