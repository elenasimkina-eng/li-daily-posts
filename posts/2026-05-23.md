## LinkedIn Post

69% of B2B software buyers chose a different vendor than they originally planned — guided by an AI chatbot.

G2 surveyed 1,076 B2B buyers in March 2026 and found that 51% now start vendor research in an AI chatbot rather than a search engine. That alone is a significant shift. But the 69% figure is the one that changes how demand generation actually works.

Most demand gen strategy is built on an assumption: brand investment at the top of funnel compounds into pipeline downstream. Awareness spend increases preference, preference increases pipeline share. That logic holds if buyers travel a relatively linear path. It breaks if an AI intermediary intercepts the journey and applies a completely different filter.

What's interesting is what that filter prioritizes. 45% of buyers in the same study said citations from software review sites were the most confidence-inspiring signal in an AI-generated recommendation — above vendor websites, case studies, or analyst content. Third-party editorial authority is what moves AI recommendations. Not brand messaging.

The structural implication: demand generation has been optimizing for the wrong visibility layer. Teams track rankings, ad impressions, landing page conversion, and MQL volume. Few are systematically tracking review site citation frequency, third-party mention coverage, or share of voice in AI-generated category responses.

G2's Answer Economy report — https://learn.g2.com/g2-2026-ai-search-insight-report — also notes that 33% of buyers purchased from a vendor they weren't previously familiar with. AI chatbots are functioning as an equalizer. That creates real competitive exposure for established brands and real opportunity for challengers.

This starts to look less like a channel shift and more like a credibility system change. The currency that drives pipeline is now citation authority in AI responses, and most attribution models don't measure it.

## Central Insight
69% of B2B software buyers chose a different vendor than they initially planned, guided by AI chatbot recommendations — and 45% say citations from software review sites are the most confidence-inspiring signal in those recommendations. This breaks the foundational demand gen assumption that building brand preference early in the funnel reliably translates into downstream pipeline. If AI intermediaries are actively redistributing purchase intent, and if the trust signals those intermediaries use are third-party editorial (review sites, citations) rather than brand-owned content, then optimizing for awareness and direct traffic may no longer be sufficient.

## Why This Matters
- Demand generation has historically operated on the assumption that brand investment at the top of funnel compounds into pipeline. That logic holds if buyers travel a relatively linear path. It breaks if an AI layer intercepts the journey and applies a different filter.
- The signals that move AI recommendations — review site citations, third-party editorial mentions — are signals that marketing teams rarely track in demand gen attribution, creating a measurement blind spot at the exact moment where vendor selection is being shaped.
- The 33% of buyers who purchased from a vendor they weren't previously familiar with suggests that AI chatbots are functioning as an equalizer — giving less-known vendors access to purchase consideration they would never have earned through traditional awareness spend.
