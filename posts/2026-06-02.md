## LinkedIn Post

The argument that AI search has become a multi-engine citation game is correct. What most GEO strategies aren't yet built around is the implication.

Kevin Indig laid out the multi-engine framing in his State of AI Search Optimization 2026 piece, and the citation data backs it directly. Per Averi.ai's analysis of 680 million citations, only 11% of domains cited by ChatGPT are also cited by Perplexity. The citation graphs across platforms are almost entirely distinct. There's no unified AI ranking signal. Each engine runs on different source preferences, credibility signals, and content formats.

Most GEO strategies are still built like SEO: identify the right structural signals, earn citations, achieve visibility. That logic works on one platform. A B2B buyer who starts research in Perplexity and completes it in ChatGPT encounters two nearly independent source landscapes, and a brand well-cited in one is largely absent from the other.

The conversion math makes this consequential. Ahrefs data shows AI search visitors generate 12.1% of signups from 0.5% of traffic. That's a 24-to-1 conversion ratio versus organic, and they're the highest-quality discovery visitors in most B2B funnels right now. Meanwhile, 96% of B2B companies are already invisible in early-stage AI discovery (2X AI Visibility Index, 2026).

Platform fragmentation makes an existing invisibility problem structurally harder, not just bigger.

Being "visible in AI search" isn't a state a brand achieves once. It's three separate citation presences, each built on different content authority signals, while buyers move between platforms mid-journey without thinking about it.

Curious whether anyone tracking cross-platform citation data is seeing the same divergence in which content formats earn citations where.
