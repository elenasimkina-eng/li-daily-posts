## LinkedIn Post

At the consideration stage — when buyers are actively evaluating vendors — 51% of what AI engines surface is social proof. Reviews. Comparison listicles. Third-party case studies. Owned product pages appear 12% of the time.

That split comes from Omniscient Digital's analysis of 43,000+ LLM citations across five models. A separate 5W PR study, based on more than one million AI prompts, found that 85.5% of all AI citations reference earned media — not brand-owned websites. AI engines cite earned media roughly five times more frequently than content a brand controls.

The assumption most demand gen programs run on: owned content creates discovery. Build the right landing page. Optimize the right keyword. Gate the right white paper.

But at the stage where vendor shortlists actually form, AI predominantly cites the things demand gen doesn't control.

Review platforms. Analyst coverage. Community discussions. Comparison articles. The same assets that have always driven peer credibility in B2B buying are now also the primary inputs to AI-generated vendor recommendations.

For cybersecurity vendors serving MSPs — where buying committees span 8+ stakeholders and peer trust anchors every evaluation — the underlying signal is familiar. What's shifted is the mechanism. Those peer credibility assets now feed directly into the AI layer where shortlists form before any demand gen motion is triggered.

Brands appearing on four or more third-party platforms are 2.8x more likely to be cited in ChatGPT responses (5W PR Citation Source Index, 2026).

This starts to look less like a content strategy problem and more like a credibility distribution problem.

## Central Insight
At the stage where vendor shortlists form, AI engines cite social proof (reviews, listicles, community content) 51% of the time and owned product pages only 12% of the time — meaning the content layer that demand gen programs primarily invest in is the least visible layer to AI-mediated discovery.

## Why This Matters
- Most demand gen programs direct budget and creative effort toward owned assets (product pages, gated content, landing pages), yet AI systems cite earned media 5× more frequently than brand-owned websites
- The content types that AI cites most heavily at consideration stage — peer reviews, third-party comparisons, community discussions — are typically owned by the analyst relations, PR, or customer marketing function, not demand gen
- For cybersecurity vendors serving MSPs — where 8+ stakeholder buying committees rely on peer credibility as the dominant trust signal — these earned assets are already critical; what has changed is that they now directly feed the AI layer where shortlists form before any demand gen motion is triggered

**AI in B2B Marketing & AI Visibility**
- 85.5% of AI citations reference earned media sources — not brand-owned websites. 5W PR's study (May 15, 2026), based on Muck Rack analysis of 1M+ AI prompts plus University of Toronto research, finds AI engines cite earned media roughly five times more frequently than brand-owned websites. Comparative listicles account for 32.5% of all AI citations overall.
Source: Omniscient Digital — https://beomniscient.com/blog/mapping-content-to-buyer-intent-in-ai-search/
