## LinkedIn Post

90% of B2B marketing organizations are using AI agents in some form. Only 23% have deployed them in full production.

That gap gets labeled a "readiness problem" — a transition phase that will close. But there's a sharper signal underneath it: the share of marketers who can prove AI ROI dropped from 49% to 41% in a single year, even as adoption kept climbing.

Adoption is accelerating. Evidence of impact is declining.

Gartner's projection that 40%+ of agentic AI projects will be canceled by end of 2027 doesn't read as a warning about future risk. It reads like a description of what's already happening: broad experimentation, thin deployment, eroding proof.

What's interesting is how much this resembles the martech stack expansion from 2019 to 2022 — accumulate access, struggle to operationalize, can't prove impact, consolidate under pressure.

This starts to look less like a technology adoption curve and more like a capability overhang.

For demand gen teams, the question isn't whether to use agentic AI. It's whether current investments are building toward production deployment or toward the 40% Gartner expects won't survive the next budget review.

Source: AI Realized Now (https://airealizednow.substack.com/p/agentic-ai-is-here-most-b2b-marketing), MarTech (https://martech.org/the-truth-about-martech-in-2026/)

## Recommended Topic
The gap between agentic AI adoption in B2B marketing and actual production deployment — and why ROI evidence is declining as adoption rises.

## Selected Angle
Skeptical

## Central Insight
B2B marketing teams are broadly adopting agentic AI while simultaneously losing their ability to prove it works — and Gartner's 40%+ cancellation prediction suggests the current deployment gap is not a transition phase but a structural pattern already in motion.

## Why This Matters
- Demand gen teams face budget pressure to scale AI investment while the evidence base for its ROI is weakening
- The 90% vs. 23% deployment gap represents significant unproductive spend concentrated in experimentation, not operation
- If Gartner's prediction materializes, it signals the AI stack will face the same consolidation pressure the broader martech stack hit in 2019–2022
