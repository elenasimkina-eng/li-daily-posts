## LinkedIn Post

The assumption behind most B2B campaigns is that the main threat is a competitor. LinkedIn's own data suggests otherwise.

40% of B2B deals are lost to indecision — not to a competitor winning. That figure comes from LinkedIn's Indie Agency Summit, analyzed by Brainlabs (https://www.brainlabsdigital.com/the-new-rules-of-b2b-targeting/). The average buying group behind that deal: 10 people.

94% of those buyers use LLMs to research vendors before any sales contact (TestimonialStar, 2026 — https://testimonialstar.com/resources/b2b-buyer-journey-llms-2026/). They arrive with shortlists already formed. The competitive comparison has usually happened before the first call.

What hasn't happened is alignment across 10 people who each ran separate AI research sessions and came to slightly different conclusions.

Most demand gen programs aren't built for that problem. They generate leads and win comparisons. Fewer than 1 in 5 B2B marketing teams track pipeline contribution rather than lead volume (Pipeline360, 555 respondents — https://www.pipeline-360.com/news/new-research-reveals-growing-gap-between-b2b-marketing-confidence-and-pipeline-performance/). The measurement gap reflects the design gap.

When a qualified buyer arrives informed and still doesn't move, it's usually not a messaging problem. It's a group-alignment problem.

## Central Insight
40% of B2B deals are lost to indecision, not to a competitor winning. With buying groups averaging 10 people, and 94% of those buyers arriving pre-informed through LLM research, the challenge has shifted from "win the comparison" to "build collective confidence across 10 stakeholders simultaneously." Most demand gen programs aren't designed for that — and fewer than 1 in 5 teams even track the metric that would reveal it.

## Why This Matters
- Demand gen teams optimizing for lead volume and competitor displacement are solving for the 60% of deal failures, while the 40% lost to indecision requires a different campaign design entirely
- Buying group size has grown structurally — cybersecurity buying committees expanded from 4–6 stakeholders (2020) to 8–10+ (2026) — making collective indecision more common, not less
- AI-informed buyers arrive with pre-formed shortlists, meaning the competitive comparison happens before first sales contact; the remaining challenge is internal buying-group alignment, not external persuasion
